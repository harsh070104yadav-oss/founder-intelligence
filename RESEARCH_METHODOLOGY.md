# Research Methodology

## Philosophy

Do not collect information. Develop understanding of mechanisms: why companies succeed or fail, how distribution is built, how models evolve, what founders overlooked or understood, and how leaders think, decide, and see the future.

## Source Hierarchy

1. **Tier 1 — Primary Issued Documents**: Regulatory filings (SEC 10-K/10-Q/8-K, SEBI, BSE/NSE), audited financials, official annual reports, investor presentations, earnings releases, shareholder letters, official company disclosures. These establish facts. Management commentary in these documents is treated as primary evidence of how leaders think.
2. **Tier 2**: Earnings transcripts, reputable journalism, academic research, recognised industry reports.
3. **Tier 3**: Founder interviews, public talks, biographies, podcasts, conference appearances. Critical for understanding leadership philosophy and future vision.
4. **Tier 4 — Public Sentiment**: X (Twitter), Reddit, forums, app reviews, social media. Used only to generate hypotheses, surface customer reactions, and identify narratives. Never independently establish financial or strategic claims.

Every material claim must have a source. Financial figures specify period, currency, metric type (revenue vs GMV vs ARR), and distinguish actual vs guidance.

## Leadership, Management Thinking & Future Vision

This is a mandatory research dimension for every significant company.

Investigate and extract:

- How founders and senior leaders think about the business, industry, and future.
- Decision-making process and capital allocation philosophy.
- What they prioritise (product, distribution, unit economics, brand, technology, people).
- Explicit statements about the future of their industry or category.
- Hiring philosophy, management systems, and organisational design.
- How they describe past mistakes and course corrections.
- Differences between what they said in previous years and what actually happened.
- Long-term thinking versus short-term optimisation.

Primary sources for this:
- Shareholder letters and annual report commentary
- Earnings call prepared remarks and Q&A
- Investor presentations and strategic updates
- Founder/CEO interviews, talks, and first-person accounts
- Proxy statements and governance disclosures (incentives, board composition)

Analyse critically. Do not turn leaders into heroes. Distinguish stated strategy from revealed strategy (what they actually did with capital, time, and attention).

## How Sentiment (especially X) Is Used

- Search X for company names, product reactions, founder posts, controversies, and recurring customer complaints or praise.
- Extract recurring themes (pricing, trust, retention, doctor experience, privacy, fulfilment).
- Treat all sentiment as unverified. Cross-check any factual assertion against Tier 1 documents.
- Include notable sentiment patterns in daily briefings under "Questions" or "Limitations," or as context for distribution and product insights.
- Do not let volume of posts or influencer opinions override official disclosures.

## Categories

- Company intelligence
- Corporate documents
- Business models
- Distribution and growth
- Technology and opportunities
- Industry structure
- Founder and leadership decisions
- Management thinking and future vision
- Failures and mistakes

## Depth

Prefer deep analysis of fewer companies over shallow coverage. Track evolution over multiple years. Update profiles with new disclosures. Always begin with primary issued documents. For every company, extract both the economic mechanisms and the leadership thinking behind them.

## Daily Process

1. Check for new primary issued documents (filings, earnings, investor presentations, shareholder letters).
2. Retrieve and analyse those documents, including management commentary on strategy and the future.
3. Supplement with Tier 2 and Tier 3 sources (especially founder/leader interviews and talks).
4. Scan X and other public sentiment for relevant reactions and themes (labelled as such).
5. Extract lessons on both business mechanics and leadership decision-making.
6. Connect to prior research and identify new hypotheses.
7. Update the knowledge base.
8. Publish the daily briefing.

## Accuracy

Never fabricate numbers. Mark unverified claims. Summarise rather than reproduce copyrighted material. Sentiment never overrides issued documents. Leadership statements are evidence of thinking, not automatically evidence of results.
