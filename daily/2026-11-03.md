## 2. Companies studied

### Teladoc Health (TDOC)
- **Background**: Founded 2002. Global virtual care platform with Integrated Care (B2B employer/health plan) and BetterHelp (primarily consumer mental health) segments. Scaled dramatically during COVID then faced utilisation normalisation, goodwill impairments, and margin pressure.
- **Business model**: Primarily access-fee (PMPM or similar) revenue from clients plus visit fees and consumer subscriptions/cash-pay. Integrated Care more stable; BetterHelp historically DTC advertising-driven, now pivoting to insurance.
- **Financial performance** (Q3 2026 primary disclosures):
  - Revenue: $605.1 million (-3% YoY). Nine months: $1,825.9 million (-3%).
  - Net loss: $26.9 million ($0.15/share).
  - Adjusted EBITDA: $79.4 million (+14% YoY).
  - Integrated Care: $398.5 million (+2%), adjusted EBITDA margin 18.8%.
  - BetterHelp: $206.6 million (-13%), adjusted EBITDA margin 2.2%.
  - Full-year 2026 guidance: Revenue $2,395–2,441 million; adjusted EBITDA $277–303 million.
  - Cash & equivalents ~$850 million; positive free cash flow in the quarter.
- **Leadership thinking and future vision** (Q3 2026 earnings release and call remarks — Chuck Divita):
  - Overarching objective: “to be the most trusted leader in virtual care. Creating value for our clients around the world and delivering outcomes that matter to patients, clients, and other stakeholders.”
  - Emphasises disciplined execution against strategic priorities: enhance Integrated Care (Teladoc One implementation, hybrid care, AI-enabled solutions), leverage scaled mental health position, international growth, operational excellence.
  - On BetterHelp: Insurance revenue exceeded expectations; progress expanding provider capacity and scaling covered services (27,000 weekly covered sessions, ~$140 million annualised run rate). Explicit that expanding insurance access can create a more durable position over time. Accepts continued cash-pay pressure from deliberate advertising reductions.
  - Acknowledges “more work ahead” while claiming actions are showing traction.
- **Important lessons**: Pure digital scale without deep clinical integration or outcomes ownership faces persistent CAC, retention, and utilisation volatility once pandemic tailwinds fade. Shifting from advertising-driven consumer acquisition to insurance/client channels improves durability but compresses near-term growth. Trust and outcomes language appears consistently, yet revealed results show slow growth and ongoing losses. Capital allocation prioritises cash generation and selective investment over aggressive pure-growth spend.

Sources: Teladoc Health Q3 2026 Earnings Release (28 Oct 2026), Form 10-Q for period ended 30 Sep 2026, Q3 earnings call transcript and related IR materials.

## 4. Business model discoveries

- Access-fee dominant model provides predictability in Integrated Care but limited pricing power once penetration is high.
- Consumer cash-pay (BetterHelp) historically high-growth via paid acquisition but sensitive to advertising efficiency and competition; insurance pivot trades growth for potentially higher retention and lower CAC.
- Vertical differences: Integrated Care more resilient; consumer segment more volatile. Positive free cash flow coexists with GAAP losses due to amortisation and prior investments.

## 5. Distribution discoveries

- B2B client distribution (employers, health plans) more durable than pure DTC advertising for virtual care once emergency demand fades.
- Insurance coverage for mental health creates a hybrid channel that aligns incentives better than pure cash-pay but requires provider capacity and credentialing investment.
- Contrasts with doctor-network (Progyny) and owned-physical (Apollo) models that embed trust more tightly.

## 6. Founder lessons

- Divita (professional CEO, not founder): Frame a clear North Star around trust and outcomes even while executing a painful mix shift. Accept near-term revenue declines from reduced advertising if unit economics improve. Prioritise disciplined execution and cash generation over narrative growth. Distinguish stated vision of virtual care leadership from revealed post-COVID normalisation and impairments.
- Broader pattern: In sensitive categories, pure digital acquisition advantages erode; durable models embed clinical relationships or measurable outcomes.

## 7. Industry intelligence

Virtual care has matured from rapid COVID expansion into a slower-growth, margin-focused phase. Players with strong B2B client relationships or specialised clinical networks are relatively more resilient than pure consumer advertising models. Mental health remains a large opportunity but faces capacity, reimbursement, and competitive dynamics. AI and hybrid care are positioned as future differentiators but have yet to reverse top-line pressure at scale.

## 8. Failures and mistakes

Rapid pure-telehealth scaling without durable engagement or outcomes produced utilisation declines, goodwill impairments, and multi-year revenue pressure. Over-reliance on paid consumer acquisition created volatility once efficiency declined. Teladoc’s experience shows the limits of “access” as a standalone moat once the acute demand shock passes.

## 9. Unexpected connections

Teladoc’s BetterHelp insurance pivot parallels broader industry moves toward more managed or reimbursed models (cf. Progyny’s employer outcomes focus). All contrast with pure DTC consumer platforms that continue to invest through margin pressure. Common thread: controlling or aligning with the care relationship and payment mechanism improves durability relative to pure advertising-driven access.

## 10. New business opportunities

- Workflow tools that help existing specialists (gynaecology, urology, mental health) demonstrate adherence or engagement to their own patients without competing for the clinical relationship.
- Privacy-first engagement layers that clinics can recommend.
- Regional or specialist-network models that avoid the pure-DTC CAC cycle.

## 11. Implications for Intima

- **Directly applicable**: Doctor- and clinic-distributed models remain structurally advantaged in trust-sensitive categories. Teladoc’s multi-year post-COVID normalisation and BetterHelp pivot reinforce that pure digital access faces higher friction on retention and unit economics.
- **Potentially applicable**: Engagement features that improve adherence once a doctor relationship exists; measurable signals valued by clinicians.
- **Long-term opportunity**: Privacy-first, workflow-compatible layers that clinics recommend without commission conflicts.
- **Not currently relevant**: Large-scale pure DTC customer acquisition or competing on broad virtual-care access fees.

Privacy-first positioning continues as a potential differentiator relative to large consumer platforms.

- When does verticalization or clinical ownership become necessary versus remaining a distribution layer?
- Can AI become load-bearing for clinical outcomes (not just support cost) in virtual care?
