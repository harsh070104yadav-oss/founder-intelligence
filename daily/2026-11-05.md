## 2. Companies studied

### Progyny (PGNY)
- **Background**: Employer-channel fertility and family-building benefits manager. Outcomes-focused Smart Cycle design, selective specialist network, Care Advocates. Expanding into pregnancy, parenting, and menopause support.
- **Business model**: Per-member or utilisation-driven fees for fertility benefit services + pharmacy benefit services. High client retention; revenue driven by covered lives and utilisation.
- **Financial performance** (primary earnings release, period ended 30 September 2026):
  - Revenue $341.3 million (+8.9% YoY from $313.3 million), driven by more clients and covered lives. Fertility benefit services $223.4 million (+10.6%); pharmacy $117.9 million (+5.8%).
  - Gross profit $83.7 million (+15%); gross margin 24.5% (from 23.2%), reflecting care-management efficiencies and lower stock-based compensation.
  - Net income $26.3 million ($0.32 diluted) vs $13.9 million ($0.15). Adjusted EPS $0.52 (from $0.45). Adjusted EBITDA $58.4 million (+6.3%); margin 17.1% (from 17.5%) as higher gross profit was partially offset by planned platform investments.
  - Clients: 607 as of 30 September 2026 (from 553 YoY). Average members ~7.18 million. ART cycles 16,723 (+4.6%). Operating cash flow $56.6 million in the quarter; TTM OCF ~$207 million. No debt; undrawn $200 million revolver. Share repurchases: nearly 3 million shares in Q3 ($82.7 million); ~11 million shares YTD 2026.
  - Full-year 2026 guidance (raised midpoint): revenue $1.365–1.385 billion (5.9–7.5% growth; 10.1–11.7% excluding prior large-client transition revenue). Adjusted EBITDA $235–240 million. Adjusted EPS $2.09–2.13.
  - 2027 setup (management expectation): >660 clients and ~8.3 million covered lives once new clients go live.
- **Leadership thinking and future vision** (direct from earnings release — Pete Anevski, CEO):
  - “Exceptionally pleased” with selling-season momentum that extended into late stages, yielding the largest single-season addition of covered lives (1.3 million) and >85 new clients.
  - Near-100% retention for 2027 plus strong demand from existing accounts to expand into more comprehensive coverage and newest services (pregnancy, parenting, menopause).
  - Results “affirm the critical importance of these benefits to employers.” Progyny is “uniquely positioned to consistently deliver meaningful value and cost trend control,” especially impactful given broader medical-cost inflation pressures.
  - Looking to 2027: “well-positioned to extend this momentum… strongest ever set-up in terms of our number of opportunities and the overall size and quality of the pipeline, as our demonstrated ability to effectively bend the cost curve positions us well.”
  - CFO Mark Livingston: strong top- and bottom-line growth with member engagement consistent with expectations; high conversion of adjusted EBITDA to operating cash flow enables continued platform investment while returning capital via buybacks.
- **Important lessons**: Outcomes-linked care design plus total-cost-of-care reporting creates high retention and expansion in an employer channel that is itself under cost-pressure. Stated strategy (bend the cost curve, expand services, careful growth) matches revealed capital allocation (platform investment + aggressive buybacks + zero debt). Prior seasonal utilisation softness has not prevented record selling-season results.

Sources: Progyny Q3 2026 earnings release (GlobeNewswire / company IR, 4 November 2026).

## 4. Business model discoveries

Utilisation-driven fertility + pharmacy benefit model continues to convert high retention and new covered lives into revenue growth. Gross-margin expansion from operational efficiencies coexists with deliberate platform investment that modestly compresses adjusted EBITDA margin. Adjacent services (pregnancy, parenting, menopause) are being adopted by existing clients, increasing relationship depth without requiring pure new-logo acquisition. Share repurchases are a material capital-return mechanism alongside reinvestment.

## 5. Distribution discoveries

Employer-channel distribution with outcomes publication and Care Advocates remains highly retentive (near 100%). Selling season success is explicitly linked by management to employers’ need to control medical-cost inflation while still offering valued benefits. Expansion of existing accounts into more comprehensive coverage is a second growth vector alongside new logos. Contrasts with pure DTC models that face higher CAC and retention pressure.

## 6. Founder lessons

Anevski consistently articulates a clear problem (underserved need, patients going into debt) and positions outcomes + cost-trend control as the durable differentiator. Capital allocation revealed in the quarter: continue investing in platform features while returning substantial cash via buybacks and maintaining a clean balance sheet. Stated long-term vision (pipeline quality, ability to bend the cost curve) is supported by the record selling-season result rather than contradicted by it. Leadership language remains measured on utilisation seasonality and focused on client-level value.

## 7. Industry intelligence

Employer fertility and women’s-health benefits remain a priority even under medical-cost inflation when the solution can demonstrate total-cost control and superior outcomes. Adjacent midlife and pregnancy services are being pulled into the same employer relationship. Pure access models continue to face different pressures (see prior Teladoc and GoodRx analyses).

## 8. Failures and mistakes

No material new failures. Prior large-client non-renewal continues to create a reported-growth headwind that management normalises by presenting organic figures. Utilisation remains within historical ranges; no evidence of structural demand erosion.

## 9. Unexpected connections

Progyny’s ability to expand existing employer relationships into pregnancy/parenting/menopause parallels broader “care continuum” moves. The explicit linkage of selling-season success to medical-cost inflation pressure is a useful reminder that cost-control proof points can accelerate adoption in benefits channels even when pure consumer DTC faces margin or retention headwinds.

## 10. New business opportunities

- Workflow or outcomes-reporting layers that help existing specialist networks demonstrate value to employers or clinics without owning the full benefit.
- Privacy-first engagement tools that clinicians can recommend inside an existing trust relationship.
- Regional or specialist-network models that avoid pure-DTC acquisition costs.

## 11. Implications for Intima

- **Directly applicable**: Doctor- and clinic-distributed, outcomes-oriented models remain structurally advantaged. Progyny’s near-100% retention and expansion of existing accounts illustrate the durability of embedding inside trusted clinical or employer channels rather than competing for pure consumer attention.
- **Potentially applicable**: Features that improve measurable engagement or provide simple outcome signals valued by referring clinicians. Adjacent continuum services can deepen relationships once initial trust is established.
- **Long-term opportunity**: Privacy-first tools that fit inside existing specialist workflows.
- **Not currently relevant**: Large-scale pure employer-benefit administration or competing on broad covered-lives scale.
