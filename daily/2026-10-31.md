## 2. Companies studied

### Hims & Hers Health (HIMS)
- **Background**: Founded 2017. Consumer platform combining telehealth and pharmacy fulfillment. Expanded internationally (including Eucalyptus acquisition). Nearly 2.9 million subscribers as of Q2 2026.
- **Business model**: Subscription-based access to consultations and products across specialties (weight management, sexual health, hair, skin, mental health, testosterone, etc.). Mix of compounded and branded products. High engagement once started.
- **Financial performance** (from Q2 2026 earnings release and call):
  - Q2 2026 revenue: $753.2 million (+38% YoY).
  - Full-year 2026 guidance raised to $3.1–3.3 billion revenue.
  - International expected to contribute at least $600 million in 2026, near breakeven on adjusted EBITDA while scaling.
  - Gross margins under pressure from branded weight-loss products and international mix. Management expects margins to remain below historical levels as these grow.
  - 2030 ambitions stated: at least $6.5 billion revenue and $1.3 billion adjusted EBITDA.
- **Distribution strategy**: Primarily direct-to-consumer digital. Growing international. Verticalizing fulfillment and personalization.
- **Competitive advantage**: Scale in personalization, closed-loop data, pharmacy infrastructure, and brand. Management claims structural efficiency from technology and verticalization.
- **Strategic decisions**: Aggressive push into weight management and international; transition to annual shareholder letter for longer-term narrative; heavy investment in AI for care experience.
- **Leadership thinking and future vision** (from Q2 2026 earnings call, Andrew Dudum):
  - Explicit vision: “proactive, preventative, always-on care affordable to everybody globally.” Goal is a unified ecosystem of doctors, specialists, coaches, pharmacists available 24/7.
  - “We are making AI load-bearing not decorative.” AI is positioned as core to personalization and efficiency, not a feature.
  - “A business can both take care of people and be profitable.” Long-term oriented mindset may create near-term GAAP volatility.
  - Customers tell them where to expand (metabolic, hormonal, sleep, longevity). Personalization is driving adherence claims (management cites higher retention on personalized GLP-1 treatments vs traditional systems).
  - Playing offense with balance sheet and verticalization to deliver quality at a price others cannot match.
  - International is both scale opportunity and path to category leadership.
- **Important lessons**: Personalization and owned fulfillment can improve adherence and retention, but mix shifts compress margins. Long-term vision is clearly articulated; short-term results show the cost of that ambition. Marketing and acquisition costs remain material.

Sources: Q2 2026 earnings call transcript (company IR), earnings release, related filings and coverage.

### Progyny (PGNY)
- **Background**: Fertility and family-building benefits management company. Serves employers. Smart Cycle design and selective specialist network + Care Advocates.
- **Business model**: Employer-sponsored benefits. Utilization-based revenue with population components. Outcomes-focused (higher live birth rates, lower multiples) rather than pure dollar caps. Expanding into smaller employers via Progyny Select.
- **Financial performance** (from recent earnings materials):
  - Q2 2026 revenue ~$350.5 million. Full-year 2026 guidance $1.36–1.385 billion.
  - High client retention (near 100% historically). Expanding relationships with existing clients.
  - Strong cash generation and share repurchases.
- **Distribution strategy**: B2B via employers and benefits consultants. Not direct consumer. Leverages HR decision-makers and measurable ROI.
- **Competitive advantage**: Published and validated clinical outcomes, selective high-quality network, Care Advocate model, and design that reduces downstream costs (multiples/NICU).
- **Strategic decisions**: Launch of Progyny Select for small fully-insured employers (expected financial impact 2027). Continued expansion of services (pharmacy, menopause, etc.). Capital return via buybacks.
- **Leadership thinking and future vision** (from Pete Anevski interviews, earnings commentary, and company materials):
  - Entered the space because of the scale of the problem (1 in 6 impacted) and lack of a real solution. Patients were going into debt for something deeply personal.
  - Leadership approach described as mission-focused: clear North Star, surround with the right people.
  - Focus on actual utilization and outcomes rather than just eligible lives counts. Utilization has been historically stable.
  - Differentiator is publishing clinical outcomes for every member (validated by third-party studies) and delivering client-level reporting.
  - Goal is to redesign the experience, not just add coverage. Integrated pharmacy (Progyny Rx) designed to reduce waste and stress.
  - Vision: equitable, high-quality care so everyone can realize dreams of family and ideal health. Expanding into midlife and menopause as natural extension.
- **Important lessons**: Outcomes data + total cost of care can create a durable B2B moat. Employer channel provides scaled distribution with lower consumer CAC but longer cycles and retention dependence. Design around the patient experience (not just reimbursement) is a source of differentiation.

Sources: Company earnings releases, conference transcripts, CEO interviews and IR materials.

## 4. Business model discoveries

- Subscription + closed-loop personalization (Hims): Can drive higher claimed adherence. Margin structure sensitive to product mix and geography.
- Outcomes-validated employer benefits (Progyny): Aligns incentives around clinical results and total cost avoidance rather than pure access or dollar caps.
- Both models show that the “product” is partly the care design and support layer, not just the treatment itself.

## 5. Distribution discoveries

- Digital-first with owned fulfillment can scale fast but faces CAC and margin pressure.
- Employer channel avoids consumer CAC and can expand via existing relationships, but depends on benefits cycle and client retention.
- Doctor/specialist network quality (Progyny) and brand trust remain powerful in sensitive categories.

## 6. Founder lessons

- Dudum: Articulate a clear long-term vision of proactive care and invest through near-term volatility. Listen to customers for category expansion. Verticalize to control quality and cost.
- Anevski: Start from a clear, large, underserved problem. Design the system around patient experience and measurable outcomes. Publish results. Expand carefully into adjacent needs (menopause, smaller employers) once the core model is proven.
- Both: Distinguish stated long-term strategy from near-term financial results. Retention and engagement metrics matter more than top-line growth alone.

## 7. Industry intelligence

Consumer health continues to attract capital and competition in weight management and adjacent categories. Fertility benefits remain a talent and retention tool for employers. Margin pressure is visible where mix shifts toward branded or international. Clinical outcomes data is becoming a competitive requirement in benefits-driven models.

## 8. Failures and mistakes

Many pure telehealth players post-COVID struggled with retention, unit economics, or quality of provider networks. Hims itself shows the cost of rapid mix shift (margin compression). Progyny’s near-100% retention suggests that outcomes and design can mitigate churn that pure access models face. Rapid expansion without owned or tightly controlled fulfillment has historically compressed margins or eroded trust.

## 9. Unexpected connections

Hims’ emphasis on personalization and adherence data parallels Progyny’s focus on published outcomes. Both are trying to move beyond pure access to measurable care quality. Apollo’s owned physical network (from prior research) offers a different path to trust and last-mile control. The common thread: the companies that control or tightly manage the care experience and supporting infrastructure appear more durable than pure marketplaces or pure digital access plays.

## 10. New business opportunities

- Clinic- or specialist-distributed tools that help doctors demonstrate outcomes or engagement to their patients (without commission conflicts).
- Privacy-first layers that fit into existing clinical workflows rather than competing as pure DTC.
- Regional models that combine digital engagement with trusted local provider or pharmacy networks.

## 11. Implications for Intima

- **Directly applicable**: Doctor-led and clinic-distributed models (Progyny’s specialist network + Care Advocate logic; Apollo’s clinical brand trust) appear structurally advantaged in trust-sensitive categories. Intima’s approach of working through gynaecologists, urologists, sexologists and IVF centres aligns with this.
- **Potentially applicable**: Personalization and engagement features that improve adherence once a doctor relationship exists (Hims-style closed-loop ideas, adapted to clinic context).
- **Long-term opportunity**: Building measurable usage or outcome signals that doctors value for their own patients.
- **Not currently relevant**: Large-scale pure DTC CAC competition or employer benefits sales cycles.

Privacy-first positioning remains a potential differentiator versus many consumer platforms.

## 12. Questions worth thinking about

- What is the true difference in unit economics and long-term retention between doctor-referred users and pure DTC users in sensitive health categories?
- How do you measure and communicate engagement or outcomes to doctors so they continue recommending without financial incentives?
- Where does owning or tightly partnering on fulfillment become necessary versus remaining a software/engagement layer?
- How do leaders’ stated long-term visions (proactive everyday care, published outcomes) map to actual capital allocation over multi-year periods?
