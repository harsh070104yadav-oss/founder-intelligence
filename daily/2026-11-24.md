## 2. Companies studied

### INVO Fertility (IVF) — Fertility clinic operator and INVOcell device, clinic-level profitability amid going concern

- **Background**: Public micro-cap (Nasdaq: IVF) healthcare services company focused on establishing, acquiring, and operating fertility clinics plus proprietary INVOcell device (enables fertilization and early embryo development in vivo within the woman’s body as alternative/adjunct to traditional IVF). Formerly NAYA Biosciences / INVO Bioscience. Operates through Fertility Clinic Services and INVOcell Device segments. Four clinics (including recent Indiana Family Beginnings and Birmingham acquisitions). CEO Steve Shum (since 2019).
- **Business model**: Clinic network generating patient revenue from ART services; INVOcell device distributed to third-party clinics and used internally. Growth via organic volume + disciplined acquisition of established, profitable independent practices. Positive clinic-level Adjusted EBITDA before corporate/public-company expenses aims to scale so overhead is absorbed across larger base. Public listing provides capital access but introduces reverse-split, dilution, and going-concern dynamics.
- **Financial context** (Tier 1: Form 10-Q for period ended 30 Sep 2026; associated 8-K earnings release 16 Nov 2026):
  - Q3 2026 revenue $2.22M (+26% YoY); consolidated clinic revenue $2.21M (+28%). Nine-month revenue $6.41M (+22%).
  - Q3 net loss from continuing operations ~$2.15M (narrowed from $3.4M); nine-month continuing loss $6.80M. Clinic-Level Adjusted EBITDA positive for seventh consecutive quarter; consolidated Adjusted EBITDA ~$(1.0)M.
  - Cash $3.7M at 30 Sep 2026. Nine-month net cash used in operations $4.73M (improved from $7.04M). Financing: ~$7.1M net warrant-exercise proceeds + $3.46M from equity purchase agreement. Shares outstanding post reverse split (1-for-4 effective 30 Oct 2026): ~1.19M.
  - Explicit going-concern: substantial doubt about ability to continue as going concern for at least one year; additional funding needed until positive cash from operations. Indiana acquisition purchase price $760k (cash + preferred).
- **Leadership thinking and future vision** (Tier 1/2: Q3 earnings release management commentary by Shum; 10-Q; prior shareholder letter Feb 2026; earnings call prepared remarks; leadership realignment Dec 2025):
  - Stated priorities: Grow patient volumes and clinic revenue organically; acquire established, profitable independent practices that preserve local physician/patient relationships; expand access via INVOcell as lower-cost/more intimate alternative; invest in people and infrastructure ahead of network expansion so corporate costs are leveraged; reach profitability through scale.
  - Capital allocation and decision-making: Disciplined acquisition of cash-flow-positive clinics rather than pure greenfield; use public markets for capital (warrants, equity lines) while improving balance sheet; realignment of CFO/CBO roles to focus execution on acquisitions and clinic operations.
  - Explicit views on the future: Independent fertility practices seek partners to serve more patients while retaining local relationships; public clinic operator can offer investors direct participation in clinic-network growth; INVOcell expands access beyond traditional high-cost IVF.
  - Stated vs revealed: Stated acquisition + organic growth and clinic-level positivity match revealed additions (Indiana, Birmingham), 28% clinic revenue growth, and seven quarters of positive clinic-level Adjusted EBITDA. Revealed: overall continuing losses, cash burn, going-concern warning, reverse split, and micro-cap valuation (~$3M market cap noted by management on ~$9M annualised run-rate) show capital intensity and limited independent durability without further financing or rapid scale. Not heroised; access narrative is consistent but constrained by balance-sheet reality.
- **Important lessons**: Clinic networks can generate positive unit economics at the practice level while corporate overhead and public-company costs create overall losses until sufficient scale. Acquisition of established practices preserves local trust but requires capital and execution discipline. Device-enabled access (INVOcell in vivo) offers differentiation but is secondary to clinic operations in current economics. Relevant parallel: specialist/clinic-embedded models in reproductive health benefit from existing clinical trust, yet under-capitalised networks face going-concern and dilution risk.

## 4. Business model discoveries

INVO’s model combines owned/operated fertility clinics (primary revenue) with a proprietary device (INVOcell) for in vivo culture as an access alternative. Clinic-level Adjusted EBITDA positivity shows viable unit economics at practice scale; overall losses reflect corporate overhead, public-company costs, and investment ahead of scale. Differs from pure employer-benefits models (Progyny) by owning clinics rather than managing benefits, and from pure DTC by embedding in physical clinical relationships. Revealed: acquisition of established practices + organic growth can drive clinic revenue while capital constraints limit speed.

## 5. Distribution discoveries

Distribution is clinic-based: patient acquisition and retention through local fertility practices and physician relationships. Acquisitions aim to preserve those local trust layers. INVOcell provides a device-mediated access path for patients/clinics seeking alternatives to traditional lab-based IVF. Relevant parallel: specialist/clinic-embedded models start with existing clinical trust rather than pure consumer acquisition; scale is required to absorb overhead and sustain the network.

## 6. Founder lessons

CEO Shum (long tenure since 2019, prior public-company finance/ops experience) has prioritised clinic network build-out via acquisition of established practices and organic volume, accepting near-term overall losses for clinic-level positivity and future scale. Capital allocation centres on disciplined deals and public-market financing. Revealed actions (clinic additions, positive clinic-level EBITDA streak, reverse split, going-concern disclosure) show both the unit-economics progress and the capital-intensity constraint. Critical distinction: stated access and relationship-preserving acquisition strategy is matched by clinic growth, but independent durability remains unproven without further capital or faster scale. Avoid heroising; success depends on converting clinic-level cash flow into overall self-sufficiency.

## 7. Industry intelligence

Fertility clinic operators face high capital intensity, regulatory/clinical quality requirements, and consolidation pressure as independent practices seek partners. Positive clinic-level economics are achievable; absorbing corporate overhead requires scale. Device innovations (in vivo culture) can expand access but are currently secondary to clinic operations. Employer-benefits and specialist-embedded models show different durability profiles. Structural demand for ART access persists amid capacity constraints.

## 8. Failures and mistakes

INVO illustrates the risk of clinic-network expansion outpacing capital availability (going concern, ongoing losses despite clinic-level positivity, reverse split). Broader pattern: under-capitalised operators in capital-intensive clinical categories face financing and dilution pressure even when unit economics improve. Historical pure-scale telehealth cases show parallel normalisation risks when growth precedes durable unit economics.

## 9. Unexpected connections

INVO’s clinic-acquisition model and emphasis on preserving local relationships parallel Progyny’s specialist-network embedding and Apollo’s physical clinical trust, but with far greater capital and scale constraints. INVOcell’s in vivo approach contrasts with pure lab-based or digital-access models. Going-concern dynamics differ from well-capitalised platforms. Relevant to reproductive access continuity under industry consolidation.

- Hybrid models combining clinic trust with selective digital support for high-intent fertility needs, reducing pure capital intensity.

## 11. Implications for Intima

- **Directly applicable**: Specialist- and clinic-distributed or clinically embedded models remain structurally advantaged for trust and retention in reproductive and women’s health. INVO’s clinic-level positivity and local-relationship focus illustrate the mechanism, while its going-concern and capital constraints highlight the scale and funding required for pure clinic networks.
- **Potentially applicable**: Simple, measurable signals (engagement, adherence, outcomes proxies) valued by referring specialists, layered on existing clinic relationships; selective device or digital adjuncts that expand access without replacing clinical trust.
- **Long-term opportunity**: Privacy-first tools that amplify trusted clinical or specialist networks rather than competing primarily on pure consumer acquisition or under-capitalised clinic ownership.
- **Not currently relevant**: Large-scale owned clinic networks without adequate capital, pure advertising-driven acquisition in high-volume categories, or full pharmacy portfolio ownership without specialist embedding.
