## 2. Companies studied

### Hims & Hers Health (HIMS) — Lilly partnership, leadership thinking, and Q3 preview

- **Background**: Consumer health platform combining telehealth and pharmacy. Co-founded by Andrew Dudum (CEO). Focus on personalization, subscriptions, international expansion (Eucalyptus), and manufacturer partnerships. Public; near 3 million subscribers as of Q2 2026.
- **Business model**: Subscription + fulfillment. Mix shifting toward branded weight-loss (GLP-1) products and international, pressuring gross margins (64% in Q2 2026 vs 76% prior year). Closed-loop data and AI for personalization. Vertical elements in pharmacy and emerging peptides.
- **Financial context** (Tier 1: prior earnings releases and 10-Qs through Q2 2026; Q3 pending):
  - Q2 2026: Revenue $753.2 million (+38% YoY). US $621.8 million (+16%); Rest of World $131.4 million (+1,641%, largely Eucalyptus). Subscribers ~2.9 million (+19%). Gross margin 64%. Adjusted EBITDA $60.3 million (8% margin). GAAP net loss impacted by legal, acquisition, restructuring.
  - Guidance (Q2 release): Q3 $880–900 million revenue; FY2026 $3.1–3.3 billion. 2030 targets ≥$6.5 billion revenue, ≥$1.3 billion Adjusted EBITDA.
  - 6 November 2026 official joint announcement (company IR / newsroom): Agreement with Eli Lilly for eligible patients to access Zepbound (tirzepatide) and Foundayo (orforglipron) at self-pay prices ($299/month and $149/month respectively) without leaving the Hims platform. Hims has never offered compounded copies of Lilly medicines and commits not to do so. Protects independent clinical decision-making. Parallels prior Novo arrangement. Reduces friction vs routing to LillyDirect.
- **Leadership thinking and future vision** (earnings releases, Q2 call, shareholder materials, interviews including CNBC Aug 2026, official statements):
  - Explicit vision: Proactive, preventative, always-on, globally affordable, customer-centered care. “Everyone is different, and today’s standard systems can’t keep up.” Closed-loop data (intake → treatment → follow-up → outcomes) as compounding advantage. AI “load-bearing not decorative”; clinicians remain in the loop.
  - Capital allocation and priorities: Short marketing payback; invest in capacity, technology, personalization, international, diagnostics. Strong cash generation supports concurrent investments. Long-term oriented; near-term GAAP volatility and margin compression from mix acceptable.
  - Decision-making: Customers drive category expansion. Trust earned by listening and delivering outcomes over years. Pragmatic adaptation to regulatory environment (shift from compounding to branded access with non-compounding commitments for Lilly/Novo products) while expanding proprietary specialties (peptides).
  - Explicit future view: 2030 targets of ≥$6.5B revenue. Platform as digital storefront for manufacturers. Price compression expected in GLP-1 category over time. AI leverage to limit headcount growth while scaling.
- **Stated vs revealed strategy**: Stated focus on personalization, manufacturer partnerships, and international matches revealed actions (Eucalyptus acquisition, Lilly/Novo deals, guidance raises, mix shift). Margin pressure and GAAP losses in recent quarters reveal the cost of the shift and scale investments. Q3 results (pending) will test whether US growth and leverage offset branded/international mix headwinds.
- **Important lessons**: Pure DTC consumer platforms can scale rapidly via brand, personalization, and manufacturer partnerships but face margin volatility, regulatory scrutiny, and retention challenges when mix shifts. Seamless branded access reduces friction but transfers more economics and control to manufacturers. Trust and adherence remain central; leadership emphasizes outcomes over pure acquisition volume.

## 4. Business model discoveries

Hims converts consumer demand into recurring subscription + fulfillment revenue, increasingly via branded manufacturer products distributed through its platform. The Lilly deal (and prior Novo) reduces abandonment friction and expands assortment while protecting clinical independence. Model produces growth but with gross margin compression from lower-margin branded/international mix. Personalization and closed-loop data aim to improve adherence and ARPU. Contrasts with high-margin automation platforms (Hinge) or employer outcomes models (Progyny).

## 5. Distribution discoveries

Primary distribution is pure consumer DTC (brand, performance marketing, app). Manufacturer partnerships provide preferential access to branded products without leaving the platform, lowering friction versus external routing. International (Eucalyptus) adds scale. Contrasts with employer/plan channels (Hinge, Progyny) or owned physical networks (Apollo). Channel partnerships with manufacturers can stabilize supply but do not eliminate CAC/retention volatility of pure consumer models.

## 6. Founder lessons

Dudum articulates a consistent long-term vision of accessible, personalized, preventative care and has adapted capital allocation and product strategy to regulatory realities (compounding constraints) while expanding partnerships and proprietary offerings. Public-company discipline is framed as forcing operational excellence. Critical distinction: ambitious 2030 targets and AI/personalization claims are forward-looking; revealed results show strong top-line growth with margin and GAAP volatility during the mix shift. Trust is described as earned through outcomes, not bought. Complementary to co-founder roles and member-first principles in other studied companies.

## 7. Industry intelligence

Consumer health platforms in weight management continue to navigate regulatory shifts from compounded to branded access, creating opportunities for seamless manufacturer partnerships. Mix shifts pressure margins. Broader digital health shows durable economics in employer/plan or clinically embedded models (Progyny, Hinge, Apollo) versus pure virtual access normalization (Teladoc). Peptides and adjacent specialties represent expandable categories under evolving regulation.

## 8. Failures and mistakes

No new material failures in primary documents this cycle. Historical lessons from pure telehealth scale (Teladoc) and control/recognition issues (GoodRx restatement) remain relevant. Hims’ path shows acceptance of near-term margin compression for long-term scale and assortment; temporary investment drag has been visible in recent GAAP results.

## 10. New business opportunities

- Tools that help consumer or specialty platforms demonstrate simple, measurable adherence or outcome signals to manufacturers or clinicians without full ownership of the relationship.
- Privacy-preserving layers that fit inside existing clinical or specialist workflows rather than requiring full platform ownership of the consumer relationship.
- Regional or hybrid models combining selective clinical presence for trust with digital access in sensitive categories.

## 11. Implications for Intima

- **Directly applicable**: Doctor- and clinic-distributed, outcomes-oriented models remain structurally advantaged. Hims leadership and the Lilly partnership reinforce that pure DTC scale can achieve rapid assortment expansion via manufacturer deals but still faces retention, margin, and trust dynamics that clinically embedded relationships mitigate more reliably.
- **Potentially applicable**: Features that improve measurable engagement or provide simple adherence signals valued by referring clinicians. Adjacent continuum services can deepen relationships once initial clinical trust is established.
- **Long-term opportunity**: Privacy-first tools that fit inside specialist or hospital workflows without requiring full platform ownership of the consumer relationship.
- **Not currently relevant**: Large-scale pure DTC subscriber acquisition, broad manufacturer cash-pay distribution of high-volume drugs, or competing primarily on advertising-driven growth.
