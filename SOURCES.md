# Sources

## Source Hierarchy (strict)

### Tier 1 — Primary Issued Documents (highest priority)
These establish material financial and strategic facts. Management commentary inside them is primary evidence of how leaders think.

- **Regulatory filings**
  - SEC EDGAR: 10-K, 10-Q, 8-K, S-1, proxy statements — https://www.sec.gov/edgar
  - SEBI / BSE / NSE filings for Indian companies
  - Equivalent filings in other jurisdictions
- **Audited financial statements** and notes
- **Official investor relations materials**
  - Annual reports (including CEO/MD&A sections)
  - Investor presentations
  - Earnings releases
  - Shareholder letters
  - Official strategic updates
- Company IR sites (examples: investors.hims.com, investors.progyny.com)
- Official company websites and press releases when they contain original disclosures
- Government and regulatory data

Always link directly to the original document. Prefer the primary PDF or HTML filing over third-party summaries.

### Tier 2 — High-quality secondary
- Earnings call transcripts (prepared remarks + Q&A reveal management thinking)
- Reputable financial journalism (with primary sources cited)
- Academic research
- Recognised industry and consulting reports

### Tier 3 — First-person leadership accounts
Critical for understanding how founders and leaders think, work, and see the future.

- Founder and CEO interviews
- Public talks, conference appearances, and podcasts
- Shareholder letters and annual report commentary
- Biographies and authorised accounts
- Proxy statements (compensation, incentives, board priorities)
- Reputable expert commentary on leadership decisions

### Tier 4 — Public sentiment and discussion (hypothesis generation only)
Used to surface questions, customer reactions, and narratives. **Never** used alone to establish revenue, margins, user counts, strategy, or other material facts.

- **X (Twitter)**: keyword, semantic, and user searches for company names, product reactions, founder statements, controversies, and customer sentiment
- Reddit, forums, and other social platforms
- App store reviews and public complaint data
- Informal discussions

Rules for sentiment sources:
- Always label clearly as sentiment / unverified.
- Cross-check any claim against Tier 1 documents.
- Track recurring themes (retention issues, pricing complaints, trust, distribution friction) rather than isolated posts.
- Note volume and direction of sentiment only as context, not as evidence of financial performance.

## Leadership Thinking Sources (priority order)

When studying how management thinks and sees the future:
1. Shareholder letters and annual report MD&A / CEO commentary
2. Earnings call prepared remarks and Q&A
3. Investor presentations and strategic updates
4. Founder/CEO interviews, talks, and podcasts
5. Proxy statements and governance disclosures
6. Historical comparison: what they said in prior years versus subsequent results

## Usage Rules

- Every material factual or financial claim must cite a Tier 1 or Tier 2 source.
- Leadership statements are evidence of thinking and stated strategy. Always distinguish them from revealed strategy (actual capital allocation, hiring, and results).
- Sentiment (X and others) may generate research hypotheses and appear in "Questions" or "Limitations" sections.
- Distinguish actual reported numbers from management guidance and from estimates.
- Specify reporting period, currency, and metric type (revenue, GMV, ARR, bookings, etc.).
- Summarise and analyse; do not reproduce large copyrighted extracts.

## Primary Document Priority

When studying a company, start with:
1. Latest 10-K / annual report (including management commentary)
2. Recent 10-Q / quarterly results and earnings release
3. Investor presentations and shareholder letters
4. Earnings transcripts
5. Founder/leader interviews and talks
6. Then Tier 4 for colour and hypotheses
